# The rules for your creator business

Your business is one fictional adult (21+) virtual creator, run from Michigan. She posts
safe-for-work content to Instagram and TikTok and explicit content to Fanvue.

This page lists the rules that most clearly reach that business, and what BAU does about
each. Every rule here was read on its official page on 2026-11-01.

This is not legal advice. BAU shows requirements and evidence. It never says you are
"compliant". Every rule waits for a lawyer's review (`legal_review_required: true`).

## The rules, and what BAU does

| Rule | What it says (in short) | What BAU does |
|---|---|---|
| **TAKE IT DOWN Act** (federal, Public Law 119-12, May 19, 2025) | Never publish intimate images, real or AI-made, of an identifiable real person without their consent. Up to 2 years in prison (3 if a minor). Platforms must remove reported images within 48 hours. | Your creator is fictional, with the same face in every post (the identity lock). No real person's likeness is allowed (`pub.likeness_consent`). Anything about minors is refused (`MINOR_REFERENCE`). |
| **Michigan intimate deep fakes law** (2025 PA 11 and PA 12, in force August 26, 2025) | Never make or share a sexual deep fake of a real, identifiable person without their written, signed consent. The person can sue, and it is also a crime. | Same controls as the line above. |
| **2257 record keeping** (18 U.S.C. 2257 and 2257A) | Producers of sexual images of *actual human beings*, including edited photos of real people, must keep ID and age records. The Justice Department's guide says computer-generated images that do not show real people are not included. | Never use a real person's photo as the starting point for an image. |
| **Fanvue's AI rules** (help centre, read November 1, 2026) | Label AI content clearly, in the bio, caption or a watermark. Never tell fans she is a real person. She must look clearly adult. No real person's likeness. No content made from other people's photos. You verify your own ID with Fanvue. | The AI label goes on every post, captions without an AI disclosure are refused, and the persona is fictional. The policy record is `examples/platform-fanvue.yaml`. |
| **Instagram and TikTok AI labels** | Both have an "AI-generated" label that cannot be changed after posting. | A week of posts turns the label on for every post (docs/WEEK.md). |

The FTC's rules on endorsements, reviews and honest advertising, and on likeness rights,
were already in BAU and stay.

## Removed (2026-11-01)

- **The two YouTube rules.** YouTube is no longer part of BAU.
- **California AB 1609.** Its own record said it covers only businesses with $500 million or
  more in yearly revenue.

## Still open: for your lawyer or accountant

These questions are not settled here, so treat them as open.

- **Does a fully fictional character fall outside these laws?** The TAKE IT DOWN Act and
  Michigan's law speak of an "identifiable" real person, and 2257 of an "actual human
  being". A lawyer should confirm that this covers your creator before any explicit post.
- **Michigan sales tax on subscriptions.** Several tax-software guides say Michigan does not
  tax digital goods such as e-books, music or streamed content. The Treasury's own guidance
  (RAB 2023-10) could not be opened on 2026-11-01, so this is *only claimed, not checked*.
  Ask an accountant. Also ask whether Fanvue collects it for you.
- **The California and EU rules still listed.** These include the companion-chatbot law (if
  she ever chats with fans through AI), the synthetic-performer ad laws and the EU AI Act's
  deepfake labels. Whether they reach a small Michigan business with fans in those places is
  a lawyer's question. They stay until one answers.
- **Fanvue's music, advertising, API and appeal rules** were not read yet
  (`examples/platform-fanvue.yaml` says so on each line).
